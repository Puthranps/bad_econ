Learn while you explore · three questions from research

Start with a prediction, run two versions, then inspect a person whose outcome changed. These papers suggest questions to investigate; this small model does not reproduce their experiments. The buttons download new starting scenarios. They do not change this recording.

01 / Cooperation

Following the neighbors

A simulation study found that an agent trained to encourage giving increased contributions from agents whose choices depended on past experience. It raises a useful question: can one person’s actions change what others are willing to do?

Kulkarni & Brunswicker (2024), Learning Nudges for Conditional Cooperation · preprint · arXiv:2409.09509.

Try: change only the share of cooperating neighbors a Reciprocator needs to see: 25% versus 75%. Follow one Reciprocator across days, alongside the contributors curve.

Which threshold makes withdrawal spread more easily? What would you expect if there were no Reciprocators?

Our agents use a simple threshold, not the paper’s learning algorithms or trained helper. Matching a pattern would not establish how people behave.

02 / Trust and rules

Remembering versus preventing

A mathematical model shows how reputation can encourage people to support institutions, which then make cooperation worthwhile in harder situations. The institution itself needs people’s cooperation to keep working.

Lie-Panis, Fitouchi, Baumard & André (2024), The social leverage effect · PNAS · DOI:10.1073/pnas.2408802121.

Try: keep the population and resources fixed; compare trust-based trade with escrow, where payment and delivery happen together. Follow food shortages and living population.

Does preventing a cheating trade change who gets enough food, or is something else limiting survival?

Zero cheating under escrow is guaranteed by our rule. The question is its downstream effect. Our escrow is free and automatic; it omits the paper’s problem of maintaining an institution.

03 / Work and distribution

Who gets the extra output?

In an agent-based economic model, productivity and workers’ bargaining power interact to shape income distribution. More output alone does not tell you how gains are divided in that model.

Rolim, Baltar & Lima (2023), Income distribution, productivity growth, and workers’ bargaining power · DOI:10.1007/s00191-022-00805-3. Open author conference version (2021).

Try: cross 12 or 18 food per hired worker with posted or negotiated wages. In all four files, the offer starts at 2, the worker’s minimum at 4, and the firm’s limit at 8.

Compare pay, firm cash, and food shortfalls. Can output rise without a worker receiving more pay?

We use one counteroffer and fixed prices and jobs, not the paper’s full economy. Hunger lowering the minimum accepted wage is our assumption, not a finding established by these papers.

How to run a downloaded scenario

Put the downloaded JSON file in the project folder. Run a new experiment, then open its HTML report. For example:

cargo run -- society --scenario lesson-escrow.json --days 60 --output lesson-escrow-results.json --html lesson-escrow.html

Use the same number of days for both versions. In the wage experiment, compare one difference at a time: posted versus negotiated at the same output, or 12 versus 18 food under the same wage rule. A changed outcome is evidence about these rules; an unchanged outcome is useful too.

What this experiment means

This is a small, separate society model. Its neighborhood layout shows who can interact; it is not a geographic reconstruction. Agents follow explicit rules for cooperation, trading, work, and survival. A free-rider or cheating trade describes an action in these rules, not a judgment about a real person.

Population changes come from the model’s food and life-cycle rules. New people receive new identities even when they occupy a previously used map slot. Children must mature before joining the workforce. The simulation does not prescribe a collapse or revolt, and its time and resource units are experimental assumptions rather than calibrated predictions.

One map tile is an agent slot. A new identity is a new person.
